# Fortune 500 Employee Benefits Analysis | SQL Case Study

## Executive Summary

This SQL case study analyzes a simulated Fortune 500-style dataset to explore how employee benefits, revenue, industry, workforce size, and healthcare coverage relate to employee tenure.

Using PostgreSQL, I analyzed company-level data to identify benefits trends, retention patterns, and workforce insights that could support business, HR, and operational decision-making.

## Business Problem

Companies use benefits packages to attract and retain employees. Leadership teams may need to understand how benefits vary by industry, company size, and revenue level, and whether stronger benefits may be connected to longer employee tenure.

This project uses SQL to analyze:

* Benefits by industry
* Tenure by healthcare coverage
* PTO and maternity leave trends
* Benefits by revenue category
* Workforce and company size patterns

## Dataset Overview

This project uses a simulated Fortune 500-style company dataset with workforce and benefits information.

The dataset includes:

* Company name
* Industry

* Number of employees
* Healthcare benefits
* Paid time off days
* Maternity leave weeks
* Average employee tenure

## Business Questions

1. Which industries offer the strongest employee benefits?
2. Do companies with healthcare benefits have higher average employee tenure?
3. How do PTO and maternity leave vary by industry?
4. Do higher-revenue companies offer stronger benefits overall?
5. How does company size relate to benefits and retention trends?

## Key Business Findings

* Companies offering healthcare benefits showed slightly higher average employee tenure.
* Technology and Healthcare companies offered some of the strongest benefits packages in this dataset.
* Larger companies tended to provide more paid time off and longer maternity leave.
* Higher-revenue companies often offered stronger benefits overall.
* Benefits patterns varied by industry, suggesting that workforce expectations and competitive offerings may differ across sectors.

## Key Workforce Insights

* Healthcare benefits may support stronger employee retention.
* Higher-revenue companies appear more likely to offer stronger benefits packages.
* PTO and maternity leave trends may reflect broader employee support strategies.
* Benefits analysis can help leaders evaluate workforce competitiveness and retention risk.

## Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, companies could:

1. Review benefits competitiveness by industry to identify potential gaps.
2. Monitor employee tenure alongside healthcare coverage to better understand retention patterns.
3. Evaluate PTO and maternity leave as part of a broader employee support strategy.
4. Compare benefits by revenue category to understand how company resources may influence workforce offerings.
5. Use benefits and tenure data together to support workforce planning and retention decisions.

This project demonstrates how SQL can be used to analyze workforce, benefits, revenue, and retention data. The findings highlight how benefits may relate to employee tenure and how business leaders can use workforce data to support retention, competitiveness, and decision-making.
